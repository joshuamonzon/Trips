Stop 1 · Nov 28 – Dec 2 · 4 nights

Tokyo A temple at dawn, Mom's frames ordered on day one, the roof over the crossing at sunset, a chef's fish market, custom pillows, vintage town, the T-bone, and a day at Mt. Fuji.

Four nights, three real days. One is out at Fuji. The other two are on foot: old Tokyo in the morning, shopping streets in the afternoon, food at night. Under each day there's now a While you're here list: nothing scheduled, just the good options within a few minutes of where you already are.

Sun29Nov

Old Tokyo at dawn, matcha and pancakes, Dad's watch, Mom's frames ordered on day one, denim, then the city from the roof at sunset

  • Jet lag wakes you at 5. Use it: Senso-ji in Asakusa at 6:30am, Tokyo's oldest temple (645 AD), the giant red lantern gate, incense you waft over yourself for luck. Thirty minutes, then the Nakamise shopping street outside it. By 10am it's a mob. At dawn it's yours. Bow once at the gate, walk the edge of the path, not the middle.
  • 9am: Hatoya Asakusa, matcha whisked in front of you, three grades to pick from. Opens 9; be there at 8:50 or it's a 30-minute line.
  • 9:45: train to Harajuku for Micasadeco, the ricotta pancakes. Opens 10 on Sundays and the line forms 30 minutes early, so this is the one place you queue. You asked for one pancake place, not four. This is it: closest to everything else today.
  • 11:30: walk Cat Street to Shibuya and Watchnian Shibuya: pre-owned Rolex, Omega, Cartier with service records, tax-free, English staff, 5.0 across 1,600 reviews. Dad's browse. Window-shop here, buy in Osaka at B.B. Garage on the 9th if the vintage piece wins; this is the modern-watch side of the comparison.
  • 12:30 lunch: Kaiten Sushi Ginza Onodera, the high-end conveyor-belt sushi, chutoro and uni. No reservations; a 20–40 minute line that moves.
  • 2pm, the stop that sets the clock: Solakzade, Harajuku, opens at 2. Mom's frames: vintage stock from the 1800s–1980s, bespoke if she wants, fitted like a tailor, eye exam and lenses on site. Order today. Custom work and lenses take about a week, so day one is the only day that works. If they're not ready when you leave Tokyo, the shop delivers: have them sent to the Dec 13 hotel, Metropolitan Marunouchi. Closed Wednesdays.
  • 3pm: Momotaro Jeans Aoyama, the Okayama selvedge denim house. Free hemming, about an hour; pick up after Shibuya Sky (open till 8) or have them send the jeans to the hotel.
  • 3:30: MixTHINKS, two floors of pre-owned designer bags, graded and priced well under Miami. Mom's stop. Ask the condition grade (N and S are like-new, A is lightly used). Passport for tax-free.
  • 4pm: Shibuya Sky, the open-air roof 750 ft over the crossing you stood in last night. Sunset is 4:28; stay till the lights come on. Book the 4pm slot the minute tickets open (they sell about a month out, and sunset goes first). On a clear day Fuji is on the horizon. The one tourist thing in Tokyo that's worth it.
  • 6:15 early dinner at Zauo Shibuya: you sit on a boat built inside the restaurant, fish your dinner out of the tank with a rod, the staff bang a drum when you land one, and they cook it however you want. Loud, goofy, great. Reserve. Done by 8, bed by 8:30. Day two of jet lag is the hard one. Nothing past 8:30.

Mon30Nov

A chef walks you through the fish market, the knife street, pillows made to your neck, teamLab, vintage town, then the T-bone

  • 10am–noon: the Tsukiji food tour with Ali, a former seafood chef (he ran a Milos) who walks you through the Outer Market: how to judge tuna by the cut, uni by the season, katsuobushi, the vendors locals use. Meet at the Shinran Shonin statue by Tsukiji Hongan-ji. Not booked yet: book the 10am slot now, it sells out. $32 a head and the food's on you: bring cash and show up hungry. Your stall list (Kagura sushi, the Maguroya Kurogin toro vendor, Kitsuneya's organ stew, Soul of Tsukiji crab legs, the oyster stall across from it, Maruni matcha, Yamacho tamagoyaki) is exactly what he walks: tell him what's on it.
  • 12:30: 20 min to Kappabashi, the kitchen-supply street: hand-forged knives engraved with your name while you wait, ceramics, the plastic food models every restaurant uses. Buy the knives now and forward them home in the suitcase, not on the last day in a rush.
  • 1:30: Nishikawa on the 7th floor of Matsuya Ginza, Japan's 450-year-old bedding house. They measure your neck and head, lay you down, test heights and fills, and build you a pillow on the spot. Walk-ins work: 30 minutes to fit, 40 to make. Go shop while it's built. Both of you. The best thing you'll bring home that isn't edible.
  • Then Ginza on foot: the 12-floor Uniqlo, Itoya stationery, the depachika food basements. Pick up the pillows on the way out. Passports out: tax-free at the register. Petite sizing is the default here.
  • 3:30 slot at teamLab Planets, 15 min away in Toyosu: a digital-art museum you walk through barefoot, wading knee-deep through water full of projected koi. Yes it's touristy. It's also one of the coolest rooms on earth. Book 3 weeks out.
  • 5:30: train to Shimokitazawa, a whole low-rise neighborhood of vintage shops, record stores, and coffee, where Tokyo's 20-somethings actually buy their clothes. Two hours of lanes, no plan. This is the walk-around-and-look-at-things part of the trip, done right: it's a town, not a mall.
  • 8pm, the Tokyo steak: T Nakameguro, 15 minutes on. Omi-beef T-bone finished over an open flame at the counter, a tasting course around it (the uni-sauce beef, tuna, lobster), sake pairing if you want it. 4.8 across 500 reviews and on the world's-best-steak lists. ¥20–30k a head. Reservation only; they answer the phone 2–5pm Japan time, or have the hotel concierge book the day you confirm. You said steak in Tokyo, once: this is the once. Backup if it's full: Shima in Nihonbashi (below).
  • Late, optional: Golden Gai is a 10-minute walk from the hotel: six alleys, 200 bars that seat six. Start with Japanese whisky at Bar Zoetrope, then hop. Only if there's gas left after a 3-hour dinner. ~¥1,000 cover each; look for bars with English signs.

Tue1DecDay trip

Mt. Fuji: a hike with the mountain filling the sky, then the sushi counter

  • Fuji's summit is closed Oct–June, so nobody climbs it in December. You hike the ridge across the lake from it instead, on the clearest-sky month of the year.
  • 7:30am Fuji Excursion train from Shinjuku, direct, 1h50. Right side faces the mountain.
  • Hike Mt. Tenjo from the lake: an hour of real climbing through forest to a viewpoint where Fuji is the whole horizon. Ropeway down. Want the big one? Mt. Mitsutoge is the 5-hour version with the best Fuji view in Japan.
  • Lunch: hoto, the fat-noodle miso stew, at Hoto Fudo. Lakeshore at Oishi Park for the reflection shot. Train back by 6.
  • 7:30pm dinner: omakase at Kyubey Ginza, the classic counter that's been feeding first-timers since 1935. Shower, change, 20 min on the subway from Shinjuku. Earned.
  • Pack tonight: big suitcases go to the front desk in the morning, overnight bags for the next two nights.

Stop 2 · Dec 2 – 4 · 2 nights · Bullet train 2h30, direct

Kanazawa Crab for lunch, geisha lanes, gold leaf, samurai streets, a thatched village in the snow, and two nights at a 200-year-old hot-spring inn.

The samurai city, never bombed, so the old districts are intact and walkable. Both nights at an onsen ryokan 25 minutes outside town: one base, no hotel-to-hotel shuffle, a soak every night, and the crab dinner on the first.

🦀
Wed2DecTravel

Fish-market lunch, the great garden, the teahouse district, samurai lanes, then out to the hot spring

  • 8am Hokuriku Shinkansen from Tokyo Station, direct to Kanazawa, in by 10:30. Left side for the mountains. Big bags forwarded straight to the Kyoto hotel, which holds them until Dec 4; you carry one overnight bag for two nights. Bag into a station locker.
  • 11am: Iki-iki Tei inside Omicho Market: the kaisendon counter. Write your name and order on the sheet by the door; peak snow crab, nodoguro, uni and sweet shrimp off the boats. Cash. Closes 1:30.
  • 12:30: Kenroku-en, one of the three great gardens of Japan, five minutes' walk. One hour, not a morning: the winter yukitsuri rope cones are up on the pines, the big pond, Japan's oldest fountain. Back in. It was cut in October as a garden day; your new notes call it a must, and an hour between lunch and the teahouses costs nothing.
  • 2:15: two hours in Higashi Chaya: the 1820s teahouse district where geisha still perform. Lattice fronts, stone lanes, shops in every house. Gold-leaf soft serve at Hakuichi (Kanazawa makes 99% of Japan's gold leaf), a matcha set in the tatami room behind Amano Tea Shop, gild a pair of chopsticks.
  • 4:15: Nagamachi, earthen walls and narrow lanes, and 45 minutes inside the Nomura house, a real samurai residence: the armor, the tea room, the tiny perfect garden. Last entry 5. In and out. It's a house, not a museum day.
  • 5:30: grab the bag, taxi 25 minutes out to Motoyu Ishiya in Fukatani Onsen (the inn also runs a shuttle; ask when booking). Yukata on, then the big outdoor bath: Fukatani's water is iron-rich and runs reddish, the real stuff. Noh stage in the garden, a 200-year-old house, almost no other foreigners.
  • Dinner in the room: kaiseki with matsuba crab four ways. Futons laid out while you take one more soak.
While you're herenothing scheduled, just the good options nearby
  • Turban Curry Kanazawa's thick black curry with a katsu, ¥1,200, five minutes from Kenroku-en. Lunch backup if the market line is long.
  • Kanazawa Castle Park next to Kenroku-en, free grounds, the rebuilt white keep, 20 minutes if you want it. teamLab's castle show was a 2023 one-off; nothing is scheduled for December 2026.
Sleep, 2 nights: Motoyu Ishiya. Night 1 with the crab dinner + breakfast (~$500 for two), night 2 room + breakfast only (~$350) so you can eat in town. Book early; crab season fills it. The kaiseki is very traditional (sashimi, whole small squid, roe); if that's a worry, ask for the crab-forward course when you book.

Thu3DecSpa night

The thatched village in the snow, sake at the brewery, duck stew and the buttery fish, back to the bath

  • 7:15 taxi from the inn for the 8:10am direct bus (75 min) to Shirakawa-go, a UNESCO village of 250-year-old farmhouses with steep gassho thatched roofs built to shed six feet of snow. Climb to the Shiroyama viewpoint for the postcard, go inside the Wada house, eat Hida beef, wander the lanes. Bus back at 1pm. A village you walk through, not a garden you stand in. The bus drops you at the gate and the whole thing is on foot.
  • Back in Kanazawa by 2:15. A sake flight at Fukumitsuya, the city's 400-year-old brewery, then anything you skipped yesterday.

Walk in at 5. Night 2 at the inn is booked without dinner on purpose: two kaiseki in a row is one too many, and these two dishes are the ones Kanazawa is known for. Closed Wednesdays; today is Thursday.
  • 8pm taxi back to the inn. One more soak, early night, ryokan breakfast in the morning.

Stop 3 · Dec 4 – 9 · 5 nights · Express 2h15

Kyoto Market streets and arcades, a sword lesson, an open-air train through the gorge, bowing deer, ten thousand gates, a Gion shopping day, two three-star kitchens, and the birthday.

Five nights, the most of any stop, because this is where the 50th happens. Fewer gardens, more streets: Kyoto's old neighborhoods are the walkable thing, and its restaurants are the high-end thing. Fall color runs latest here, so early December still catches red maples with the crowds gone.

🗡️
Fri4DecTravel

Into Kyoto, the market street, the arcades, build a watch, a samurai sword lesson, sushi with your shoes off

  • Ryokan breakfast, one last soak, 9:30 taxi to the station for the 10:15 Thunderbird express along Lake Biwa. In by 12:30. Bags already at the hotel.
  • 2pm: Nishiki Market, "Kyoto's kitchen": five blocks of pickles, tofu doughnuts, knife shops, grilled everything. Graze for lunch. It's two minutes from the hotel.
  • Straight into Teramachi and Shinkyogoku, two covered arcades running north off the market: vintage shops, kimono, tea, sneakers, the second-oldest arcade in Japan. An hour and a half of walking and looking.
  • 4:45: KUOE, the Kyoto watch brand that lets you build your own, case, dial, hands, strap, at the flagship two minutes from the hotel. Closed Tuesdays. A watch built in Kyoto, on the trip, for Dad. Thirty minutes.
  • Evening: Samurai Kembu: a real sword-dance school where you put on the hakama, learn to draw and cut with a katana from a master, then watch the performance. 90 minutes. Touristy in the best way. Dad will talk about this for years.
  • 8:30 dinner: Tai Sushi, five minutes from the hotel: shoes off, a family counter, the otoro and uni locals send you to, honest prices. 5–10pm, closed Thursdays and Sundays. Reserve, or walk in by 8. Then Pontocho, the lantern-lit lane five feet wide along the river, on the way home.

Sun6DecDay trip

Nara the right way round: lanterns first, up the grass mountain, down through the deer to the giant Buddha; sake street; the mountain of gates at dusk

  • 45 min to Kintetsu Nara, then the bus up to Kasuga Taisha first: the lantern-lined cedar path (3,000 stone lanterns) and the vermilion shrine, 30 minutes. Your note was right: start at the top and walk down. The deer up here are calmer, the crowds haven't arrived, and the whole day is downhill.
  • 11:15: Mt. Wakakusa, a 25-minute climb up a grass hill (¥150) to the view over all of Nara, deer grazing the slope. Not a hike, a walk; it's the active bit of the day.
  • 12:30: down through the deer park. Buy the crackers here, where the 1,200 wild deer bow for them, then Todai-ji: the largest wooden building on earth, a 50-ft bronze Buddha from 752. Lunch on Higashimuki, Nara's covered shopping street, on the way back to the train. Sacred messengers of the gods. They will also eat your map. This is the active kind of park: you're the entertainment.
  • Back via Fushimi, Kyoto's sake district: 20 breweries on a willow canal, one tasting counter with 18 of them.
  • Fushimi Inari at dusk: 10,000 orange torii gates up a mountain. Everyone stops at the first 200 yards. Keep going 30 minutes and you're alone in a cedar forest as the lanterns come on. Free, open all night.
  • Dinner on Kiyamachi, the bar-and-restaurant street behind the hotel. No reservation, walk until something looks right.

Mon7Dec

The Gion shopping day: a workwear atelier, the pagoda lane, the stone streets, the temple on stilts at golden hour, then the oldest three-star in Japan

  • 11am: Taiga Takahashi, the Kyoto label that remakes 1900s–1940s American workwear with Japanese fabric, in a traditional machiya in Gion with a gallery upstairs. Sizing runs boxy; try everything on. Then the Hanamikoji and Shijo Gion shops.
  • Lunch in Gion, then the Yasaka backstreets on foot: Ishibe-koji, Nene-no-michi, the lanes between the temples where the old houses are shops and cafés now.
  • 1:45: Yasaka Pagoda, the five-story pagoda rising over the stone lane: the postcard of Kyoto. Ten minutes, the photo. Then the stone lanes of Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka up the hill, every house a shop. Kimono rental shops sit at the bottom of the hill; if Mom wants a practice run before tomorrow's shoot, this is the walk.

Before you go

Ten things to know, then stop reading and go.

Three apps, one card

Flush finds the nearest public bathroom (Japan has them everywhere; the app knows which ones are clean). Kuli Kuli is the translation app that explains the context, not just the words: menus, signs, what the shopkeeper actually meant. GO Taxi is Japan's Uber: card on file, English, no phone call. Add a Suica card to your iPhone Wallet before you leave and tap it on every train, bus, locker and vending machine in Japan. In Osaka, add the one-day metro pass on top of Suica for the day you're crossing town (Dec 10); it pays for itself by the third ride. Korea: Naver Map or KakaoMap for directions (Google can't route there), Kakao T for taxis.

Shrine manners, two rules

Bow once before you walk through a torii gate, and once again on the way out. Don't walk down the middle of the path: that lane is for the gods, so keep to one side. At the water basin, rinse your left hand, then right, then pour a little into your left palm for your mouth; don't drink from the ladle. At the hall: a coin in the box, two bows, two claps, one bow. Temples (Buddhist) are the same minus the claps. That's all anyone expects of you; everything else is forgiven.

The breakfast chains and the dollar stores

Every city has these, and they're where Japan actually eats before 9. Komeda Coffee (opens 7): buy one drink and the toast-and-egg breakfast is free. Matsuya: every Japanese breakfast, egg on rice, from ¥400. Nakau: noodles for breakfast. Sukiya: build-your-own rice bowl, open 23 hours. Bikkuri Donkey: a burger chain that does a Denny's-style toast, egg, bacon and coffee from 8. For the house: Daiso, Seria and 3Coins are the ¥100–300 stores (Dollar Tree with design sense; this is where the room-scent, kitchen and stationery haul comes from), and Standard Products is Daiso's grown-up line. Don Quijote is the 24-hour everything store; the Shibuya one is seven floors.

The bag rhythm

Twice on this trip you live out of a small bag while the big suitcases travel ahead. Dec 2–4: the Tokyo hotel ships the suitcases to Kyoto (Kyoto holds them); you carry a backpack or duffel for the two ryokan nights outside Kanazawa. Dec 9: you carry the suitcases yourself once, Kyoto to Osaka, a 15-minute train. Dec 11–13: Osaka ships them to the Dec 13 Tokyo hotel and you fly to Seoul with carry-ons only. Pack the small bags on purpose: three days of clothes, trail shoes for the Seoul hike, toiletries, chargers, meds, and room for the K-beauty haul. Inns provide robes and slippers. Forwarding is ~$20 a bag, arrives the next afternoon, and every hotel desk does it. Mom's frames from Solakzade ship to the Dec 13 hotel too.

Korea paperwork & phones

US passports don't need a K-ETA visa waiver through Dec 31, 2026, but you do need the free online e-Arrival Card, filled out within 72 hours before landing (so Dec 8–11; the birthday-night reminder is in the itinerary). Your Japan eSIM almost certainly won't cover Korea: buy a separate Korea eSIM or turn on roaming for three days. Korea runs on cards, but carry a little won (₩) for the market stalls and the hanbok shop.

The Seoul clinic days, honestly

Dec 11–12 are now a dentist, a hospital checkup and three clinics. Three things to do ahead: book SNUH first (4–6 weeks, no walk-ins, fasting from midnight the night before); send Lucid Dental photos of Dad's teeth now so they can tell you whether a full set fits in two visits and what it costs (their listed price for a full set of laminates is ~$7,000; this is not a thing to decide on the day); tell Reberry, Hana and Park Jun what you want in the first WhatsApp message, because they quote and schedule from that. Botox is a 20-minute, no-downtime appointment; skip anything else with needles into the face the day before a flight. An isolation tank was on the wish list and there's no float studio in Gangnam worth the taxi; the oxygen chamber is the closest thing and it's already in.

Money & phones, Japan

Japan still runs on more cash than you'd expect (Iki-iki Tei, Imo Pippi, Gion Komori and most market stalls are cash only). Pull yen from 7-Eleven ATMs (US cards work, good rates). Tipping does not exist, in either country. Buy a Japan eSIM (Ubigi or Airalo) before flying. Google Translate's camera reads menus in both countries; Kuli Kuli explains them.

Shopping tax-free

Japan: show your passport at any store with the Tax-Free sign for 10% off on purchases over ¥5,000: Uniqlo, department stores, drugstores, Nishikawa, Momotaro, MixTHINKS, Watchnian, KUOE, Don Quijote, most boutiques. Petite sizing is the default. Korea: same idea at Olive Young and the department stores, refund at the airport kiosk. Leave room in the carry-on for Seoul; the suitcases are waiting in Tokyo for the final pack. Knives go in checked bags only.

Weather & jet lag

Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka: 40–55°F, dry, sunny. Kanazawa and the ryokan: 35–45°F, grey, rain or first snow. Seoul: colder, 25–40°F and windy; real coat, hat, gloves, and the sunrise hike will be below freezing. Waterproof shoes, layers. Japan is 14 hours ahead of Miami, Korea the same. On the Montréal–Tokyo leg, eat, then sleep 7 hours (it takes off at 1:40am Tokyo time). First two days: sunlight, walk, no nap after 3pm, bed by 10. By day 3 you're fine.
