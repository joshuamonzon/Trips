Stop 1 · Nov 28 – Dec 2 · 4 nights

Tokyo A temple at dawn, a chef's fish market, custom pillows, catch-your-own-dinner, a vintage crawl, and a day at Mt. Fuji.

Four nights, three real days. One is out at Fuji. The other two are on foot: old Tokyo in the morning, shopping streets in the afternoon, food at night.

Sat28NovArrival

Land, breathe, ramen, sleep

  • Land at Narita 4:30pm, through immigration by 5:30. Narita Express straight to Shinjuku, 80 min, reserved seats, luggage racks. Hotel by 7:15. Narita is the far airport, 40 miles out. The train beats a car on both time and price. Buy N'EX tickets at the counter after customs.

Sun29Nov

Old Tokyo at dawn, the knife street, a chef walks you through the fish market, pillows made to your neck, then dinner you catch yourself

  • Jet lag wakes you at 5. Use it: Senso-ji in Asakusa at 6:30am, Tokyo's oldest temple (645 AD), the giant red lantern gate, incense you waft over yourself for luck. Thirty minutes, then the Nakamise shopping street outside it. By 10am it's a mob. At dawn it's yours.
  • Walk 10 min to Kappabashi, the kitchen-supply street: hand-forged knives engraved with your name while you wait, ceramics, the plastic food models every restaurant uses. Buy the knives now and forward them home in the suitcase, not on the last day in a rush.
  • 10am–noon: the Tsukiji food tour with Ali, a former seafood chef (he ran a Milos) who walks you through the Outer Market: how to judge tuna by the cut, uni by the season, katsuobushi, the vendors locals use. Meet at the Shinran Shonin statue by Tsukiji Hongan-ji. 5.0 across 50 reviews, $32 a head, and the food's on you: bring cash and show up hungry. Book the 10am slot now; it sells out.
  • 12:30: Nishikawa on the 7th floor of Matsuya Ginza, Japan's 450-year-old bedding house. They measure your neck and head, lay you down, test heights and fills, and build you a pillow on the spot. Walk-ins work: 30 minutes to fit, 40 to make. Go shop while it's built. Both of you. The best thing you'll bring home that isn't edible.
  • Then Ginza on foot: the 12-floor Uniqlo, Itoya stationery, the depachika food basements. Pick up the pillows on the way out. Passports out: tax-free at the register. Petite sizing is the default here.
  • 3pm slot at teamLab Planets, 15 min away in Toyosu: a digital-art museum you walk through barefoot, wading knee-deep through water full of projected koi. Yes it's touristy. It's also one of the coolest rooms on earth. Book 3 weeks out.
  • 5:45pm early dinner at Zauo Shibuya: you sit on a boat built inside the restaurant, fish your dinner out of the tank with a rod, the staff bang a drum when you land one, and they cook it however you want: sashimi, grilled, tempura. Loud, goofy, great. Reserve. Done by 7:30, bed by 8. Day two of jet lag is the hard one. Nothing past 8pm.

Mon30Nov

The vintage crawl: Harajuku to Aoyama on foot, a whole second-hand neighborhood, yakitori alley, tiny bars

  • 10:30 start in Harajuku's back streets (Cat Street, not the main drag). Solakzade: the vintage eyewear shop, frames from the 1800s to the 1980s, fitted like a tailor. Dad's stop.
  • Five minutes on: MixTHINKS, two floors of pre-owned designer bags, every house you can name, graded and priced well under Miami. 5.0 across 1,400 reviews. Mom's stop. Ask the condition grade (N and S are like-new, A is lightly used). Passport for tax-free.
  • Walk Omotesando to Aoyama: Momotaro Jeans, the Okayama selvedge denim house. They hem for free on the spot; it takes about an hour, so leave the jeans and go eat.
  • Lunch: Katsudon-ya Zuicho in Shibuya, 20 min away. One dish, eight seats, a short line that moves. Add the extra egg. Then back to Aoyama for the hemmed jeans.
  • 3:30: train 20 min to Shimokitazawa, a whole low-rise neighborhood of vintage shops, record stores, and coffee, where Tokyo's 20-somethings actually buy their clothes. Two hours of lanes, no plan. This is the "walk around and look at things" part of the trip, done right: it's a town, not a mall.
  • Night: yakitori under the train tracks at Omoide Yokocho, then Golden Gai: six alleys, 200 bars that seat six people each. Start with Japanese whisky at Bar Zoetrope, then hop. Look for bars with English signs; ~¥1,000 cover each.

Tue1DecDay trip

Mt. Fuji: a hike with the mountain filling the sky, then the sushi counter

  • Fuji's summit is closed Oct–June, so nobody climbs it in December. You hike the ridge across the lake from it instead, on the clearest-sky month of the year.
  • 7:30am Fuji Excursion train from Shinjuku, direct, 1h50. Right side faces the mountain.
  • Hike Mt. Tenjo from the lake: an hour of real climbing through forest to a viewpoint where Fuji is the whole horizon. Ropeway down. Want the big one? Mt. Mitsutoge is the 5-hour version with the best Fuji view in Japan.
  • Lunch: hoto, the fat-noodle miso stew, at Hoto Fudo. Lakeshore at Oishi Park for the reflection shot. Train back by 6.
  • 7:30pm dinner: omakase at Kyubey Ginza, the classic counter that's been feeding first-timers since 1935. Shower, change, 20 min on the subway from Shinjuku. Earned.
  • Pack tonight: big suitcases go to the front desk in the morning, overnight bags for the next two nights.

Stop 2 · Dec 2 – 4 · 2 nights · Bullet train 2h30, direct

Kanazawa Crab for lunch, geisha lanes, gold leaf, samurai streets, a thatched village in the snow, and two nights at a 200-year-old hot-spring inn.

The samurai city, never bombed, so the old districts are intact and walkable. Both nights at an onsen ryokan 25 minutes outside town: one base, no hotel-to-hotel shuffle, a soak every night, and the crab dinner on the first.

🦀
Wed2DecTravel

Fish-market lunch, the teahouse district, samurai lanes, then out to the hot spring

  • 8am Hokuriku Shinkansen from Tokyo Station, direct to Kanazawa, in by 10:30. Left side for the mountains. Big bags forwarded straight to the Kyoto hotel, which holds them until Dec 4; you carry one overnight bag for two nights. Bag into a station locker.
  • Lunch at a counter inside Omicho Market: peak snow crab season, plus uni and sweet shrimp off the boats.
  • Two hours in Higashi Chaya: the 1820s teahouse district where geisha still perform. Lattice fronts, stone lanes, and shops in every house. Gild a pair of chopsticks at a gold-leaf workshop (Kanazawa makes 99% of Japan's gold leaf), eat the gold-leaf ice cream.
  • 3:30: Nagamachi, earthen walls and narrow lanes, and 45 minutes inside the Nomura house, a real samurai residence: the armor, the tea room, the tiny perfect garden. In and out. It's a house, not a museum day.
  • 5pm: grab the bag, taxi 25 minutes out to Motoyu Ishiya in Fukatani Onsen (the inn also runs a shuttle; ask when booking). Yukata on, then the big outdoor bath: Fukatani's water is iron-rich and runs reddish, the real stuff. Noh stage in the garden, a 200-year-old house, almost no other foreigners.
  • Dinner in the room: kaiseki with matsuba crab four ways. Futons laid out while you take one more soak.
Sleep, 2 nights: Motoyu Ishiya. Night 1 with the crab dinner + breakfast (~$500 for two), night 2 room + breakfast only (~$350) so you can eat in town. Book early; crab season fills it. The kaiseki is very traditional (sashimi, whole small squid, roe); if that's a worry, ask for the crab-forward course when you book.

Thu3DecSpa night

The thatched village in the snow, sake at the brewery, the buttery fish, back to the bath

  • 7:15 taxi from the inn for the 8:10am direct bus (75 min) to Shirakawa-go, a UNESCO village of 250-year-old farmhouses with steep gassho thatched roofs built to shed six feet of snow. Climb to the Shiroyama viewpoint for the postcard, go inside the Wada house, eat Hida beef, wander the lanes. Bus back at 1pm. A village you walk through, not a garden you stand in. The bus drops you at the gate and the whole thing is on foot.
  • Back in Kanazawa by 2:15. A sake flight at Fukumitsuya, the city's 400-year-old brewery, then anything you skipped yesterday.

  • 8pm taxi back to the inn. One more soak, early night, ryokan breakfast in the morning.

Stop 3 · Dec 4 – 9 · 5 nights · Express 2h15

Kyoto Market streets and arcades, a sword lesson, an open-air train through the gorge, bowing deer, ten thousand gates, a Gion shopping day, two three-star kitchens, and the birthday.

Five nights, the most of any stop, because this is where the 50th happens. Fewer gardens, more streets: Kyoto's old neighborhoods are the walkable thing, and its restaurants are the high-end thing. Fall color runs latest here, so early December still catches red maples with the crowds gone.

🗡️
Fri4DecTravel

Into Kyoto, the market street, the arcades, build a watch, a samurai sword lesson

  • Ryokan breakfast, one last soak, 9:30 taxi to the station for the 10:15 Thunderbird express along Lake Biwa. In by 12:30. Bags already at the hotel.
  • 2pm: Nishiki Market, "Kyoto's kitchen": five blocks of pickles, tofu doughnuts, knife shops, grilled everything. Graze for lunch. It's two minutes from the hotel.
  • Straight into Teramachi and Shinkyogoku, two covered arcades running north off the market: vintage shops, kimono, tea, sneakers, the second-oldest arcade in Japan. An hour and a half of walking and looking.
  • 4:45: KUOE, the Kyoto watch brand that lets you build your own, case, dial, hands, strap, at the flagship two minutes from the hotel. Closed Tuesdays. A watch built in Kyoto, on the trip, for Dad. Thirty minutes.
  • Evening: Samurai Kembu: a real sword-dance school where you put on the hakama, learn to draw and cut with a katana from a master, then watch the performance. 90 minutes. Touristy in the best way. Dad will talk about this for years.
  • Late dinner in Pontocho, the lantern-lit lane five feet wide along the river, out the back of the hotel.
Sleep, 5 nights: Hotel Resol Kawaramachi Sanjo (~$225, central, public bath, Nishiki and the arcades out the front door, Pontocho out the back) or a restored machiya townhouse to yourselves (~$275). Gion is a 10-min walk either way.

Sun6DecDay trip

Bowing deer, the giant Buddha, sake street, the mountain of gates at dusk

  • 45 min to Nara. Buy deer crackers: 1,200 wild deer roam the park and bow for food. Sacred messengers of the gods. They will also eat your map. This is the active kind of park: you're the entertainment.
  • Todai-ji: the largest wooden building on earth, a 50-ft bronze Buddha from 752. Then the lantern-lined cedar path to Kasuga Shrine, 30 minutes, and lunch on Higashimuki, Nara's covered shopping street, on the way back to the train.
  • Back via Fushimi, Kyoto's sake district: 20 breweries on a willow canal, one tasting counter with 18 of them.
  • Fushimi Inari at dusk: 10,000 orange torii gates up a mountain. Everyone stops at the first 200 yards. Keep going 30 minutes and you're alone in a cedar forest as the lanterns come on. Free, open all night.
  • Dinner on Kiyamachi, the bar-and-restaurant street behind the hotel. No reservation, walk until something looks right.

Stop 4 · Dec 9 – 11 · 2 nights · Bullet train 15 min

Osaka The city's kitchen, the knife street, a mile of covered arcade, vintage watches, whisky at the source, a mall city, and the retro neighborhood under the tower.

Two nights now, and the whole stay is on foot: Osaka is markets, arcades, and neon, which is exactly the brief. Motto of the city: "eat till you drop."

🔪
Wed9DecTravel

Osaka's kitchen, the knife street, the arcade, Amerikamura, neon

  • 9:30 Shinkansen, 15 minutes. Bags come with you this once. Drop them at the hotel by 10:30.
  • 11am: Kuromon Market, 600 meters of covered market: grilled king-crab legs, otoro, wagyu skewers, oysters, eaten standing up. The good stalls sell out by 1; go hungry.
  • 12:30: Sennichimae Doguyasuji, five minutes on: 150 meters of professional kitchen shops. Knives (they'll engrave a name), takoyaki pans, ceramics, the plastic display food every restaurant window uses. Closes at 6.
  • 2pm: Shinsaibashi-suji, a 600-meter covered arcade: Uniqlo, drugstores, the giant Don Quijote, every brand. Tax-free with passport. It pours into Amerikamura, Osaka's vintage-and-streetwear quarter, on the west side.
  • 3:30: B.B. Garage in Amerikamura: vintage Americana out front, and at the back of the shop, the vintage watch counter, from King Seikos to Swiss. 4.9 across a thousand reviews; the staff walk you through every defect and don't push. Ask whether it's been serviced and whether the dial and hands are original. The watch stop of the trip. Open till 8, closed Wednesdays, and Dec 9 is a Wednesday, so if the shutter's down, it's Thursday at 4 instead (below).
  • 6pm: Dotonbori at dusk: the canal, the giant Glico running man, the 3-D crab signs, a hundred food stalls. Kushikatsu, okonomiyaki, takoyaki, standing up.
Sleep, 2 nights: Cross Hotel Osaka (~$210, one block from Dotonbori, five minutes from Amerikamura) or Hotel Royal Classic (~$220, the one that looks like a kabuki theater, on Namba Station).

Stop 5 · Dec 11 – 13 · 2 nights, 3 days · Flight 2h

Seoul An IV for the jet lag, an oxygen chamber, the Gangnam skin menu, a color analysis, a scalp spa, custom skincare, a sunrise hike up the city wall, ginseng chicken, and the palace in silk.

Three days built like a recovery camp with shopping in between: Gangnam is where the clinics are, so that's where you sleep. Carry-on only; the suitcases are already on their way to Tokyo. This isn't the sit-in-a-bathhouse version of Seoul. It's the one where you leave in better shape than you arrived.

💉
Fri11DecTravel · Recovery

Land, IV drip, oxygen chamber, the tree-lined boutique street, Korean beef

  • 7am airport express from Namba to Kansai (45 min). Morning flight Kansai → Incheon, 2 hours; through immigration by noon with the e-Arrival Card you filled out on the 8th. AREX train plus one subway change to Gangnam, 75 min. Drop bags at 1.
  • 2pm: AGJ Hana Clinic in Apgujeong, a doctor-led clinic that works with visitors over WhatsApp: a longevity consult, then a NAD+ or glutathione IV in a recliner, 90 minutes. The jet-lag reset. You walk in off a flight and walk out with your head clear.
  • 4pm: O2ON in Sinsa, 10 minutes away: 60 minutes of medical-grade hyperbaric oxygen. Same chamber the plastic-surgery clinics charge $250 for, at a third of the price, with a nicer front desk.
  • 5:30: Garosu-gil, the tree-lined boutique street a 10-minute walk from the chamber: Korean designers, concept stores, cafés. Olive Young for the first K-beauty haul (tax-free with passport).
  • 8pm: hanwoo Korean BBQ, the premium native beef, grilled at your table in Sinsa or Apgujeong (ask the hotel for their pick). Then the hotel's hot tub, cold plunge, sauna, bed. Hot, cold, hot: the contrast-bathing round that flushes the flight out of your legs.
Sleep, 2 nights: Andaz Seoul Gangnam (~$300, pool, sauna and hot tub on site, direct subway access from B2, every clinic within 15 minutes). Gangnam is a deliberate move from the old-town hotel: three of the four Seoul days are here. Alt, north side: Nine Tree Insadong, ~$230.

Before you go

Seven things to know, then stop reading and go.

The bag rhythm

Twice on this trip you live out of a small bag while the big suitcases travel ahead. Dec 2–4: the Tokyo hotel ships the suitcases to Kyoto (Kyoto holds them); you carry a backpack or duffel for the two ryokan nights outside Kanazawa. Dec 9: you carry the suitcases yourself once, Kyoto to Osaka, a 15-minute train. Dec 11–13: Osaka ships them to the Dec 13 Tokyo hotel and you fly to Seoul with carry-ons only. Pack the small bags on purpose: three days of clothes, trail shoes for the Seoul hike, toiletries, chargers, meds, and room for the K-beauty haul. Inns provide robes and slippers. Forwarding is ~$20 a bag, arrives the next afternoon, and every hotel desk does it.

Korea paperwork & phones

US passports don't need a K-ETA visa waiver through Dec 31, 2026, but you do need the free online e-Arrival Card, filled out within 72 hours before landing (so Dec 8–11; the birthday-night reminder is in the itinerary). Your Japan eSIM almost certainly won't cover Korea: buy a separate Korea eSIM or turn on roaming for three days. Download Naver Map or KakaoMap before you land; Google Maps can't do walking or driving directions in Korea. Korea runs on cards, but carry a little won (₩) for the market stalls and the hanbok shop.

The Seoul clinics, honestly

Everything on Dec 11–12 is single-session, zero-downtime, and done by the thousand on visitors every week: IV drips, the oxygen chamber, color analysis, the scalp spa, and the skin lifting are all walk-out-looking-normal treatments. Two things to do ahead: tell each clinic what you want in the first WhatsApp message (they quote and schedule from that), and have Mom decide the Reberry menu before the trip, because the price ranges from a $150 LDM glow to a $2,000 Thermage. Skip anything with needles into the face the day before a flight. Tongin, the Korean-medicine clinic from the original list, is closed Fri–Sun, which is exactly when you're there, so it's out.

Money & phones, Japan

Japan still runs on more cash than you'd expect. Pull yen from 7-Eleven ATMs (US cards work, good rates). Tipping does not exist, in either country. Add a Suica card to your iPhone Wallet before you leave and tap it on every train, bus, and vending machine. Buy a Japan eSIM (Ubigi or Airalo) before flying. Google Translate's camera reads menus in both countries.

Shopping tax-free

Japan: show your passport at any store with the Tax-Free sign for 10% off on purchases over ¥5,000: Uniqlo, department stores, drugstores, Nishikawa, Momotaro, MixTHINKS, KUOE, most boutiques. Petite sizing is the default. Korea: same idea at Olive Young and the department stores, refund at the airport kiosk. Leave room in the carry-on for Seoul; the suitcases are waiting in Tokyo for the final pack. Knives go in checked bags only.

Two closed-day traps

B.B. Garage in Osaka is closed Wednesdays, and Dec 9 is a Wednesday; the Thursday 4pm backup is in the plan. KUOE in Kyoto is closed Tuesdays; it's scheduled for Friday the 4th. Zuicho is closed Sundays; it's on Monday. Amore Seongsu is closed Mondays; it's on Saturday. If any day shifts, re-check these four.

Weather & jet lag

Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka: 40–55°F, dry, sunny. Kanazawa and the ryokan: 35–45°F, grey, rain or first snow. Seoul: colder, 25–40°F and windy; real coat, hat, gloves, and the sunrise hike will be below freezing. Waterproof shoes, layers. Japan is 14 hours ahead of Miami, Korea the same. On the Montréal–Tokyo leg, eat, then sleep 7 hours (it takes off at 1:40am Tokyo time). First two days: sunlight, walk, no nap after 3pm, bed by 10. By day 3 you're fine.
